# Novagentica voice — extended tone-of-voice guide

> Canonical source: the Novagentica Design System (`guidelines/brand.md`, Voice section,
> and the brand-voice card). This file is the worked, example-heavy version for deck writing.
> Client names below are placeholders — never ship a deck with another customer's name in it.

## The register

Restrained, editorial, executive. The deck sounds like a principal who has done the work,
not a vendor who wants the work. Every sentence earns its place; nothing is decoration.

- **Declarative fragments ending in periods.** Not "We're excited to explore…" but "Three MVPs. One held in reserve."
- **"We / your", never third person.** "Your team already built the agent. We put it under authority."
- **Never hype.** No "cutting-edge", "revolutionary", "game-changing", "unlock". No exclamation marks. No emoji.
- **Numbers are concrete claims tied to outcomes**, not garnish: "Routine in-policy POs: 3–5 days → under 1 hour." A number without a consequence is cut.
- **`™` on module names**: Architect™, Control™, CDAP™ — first mention per artefact is enough.

## The two-beat headline

The signature move: a statement, then a quieter second line that lands the point.
The first beat is ink; the second beat is muted or italic. Serif, large, tight.

```
Not the agent.
The governance.
```

```
Start before the contract.
Without commitment or prejudice.
```

Antithesis and parallelism do the persuasion — old vs new, theirs vs ours, risk vs proof:

- "The work is yours. The governance is ours."
- "Not another copilot. A passport office."
- "Agents will make mistakes. Yours will have to show their papers."

## Eyebrows (kickers)

ALL CAPS, dot-separated, tracked wide (`0.16em`), muted color, 10pt. Pattern:
`[NUMERAL ·] [CLIENT OR CONTEXT] · [TOPIC]`

- `NOVAGENTICA · USE CASE FIT`
- `III · [CLIENT] · PROCUREMENT & SAP DOMAIN`
- `IV · DELIVERY · FIRST 6 WEEKS`

The eyebrow names the chapter; the headline makes the argument. Never restate one in the other.

## The close

Section and deck closers use a crimson top rule + a serif line that resolves the two-beat:
short, settled, no call-to-action language ("reach out", "let's connect" are banned).
A bold crimson ending line is allowed once per deck at most.

- "The work is yours. The governance is ours."
- "NO PASSPORT = NO EXECUTION" (crimson, caps — the one shout the brand permits)

## Do / don't

| Don't write | Write |
|---|---|
| "We're thrilled to present our innovative solution!" | "The proposal. Three MVPs, one reserve." |
| "Leverage cutting-edge agentic AI capabilities" | "Agents that show their papers before they act." |
| "Significant efficiency improvements" | "3–5 days → under 1 hour; 40–60% straight-through." |
| "Next steps: schedule a follow-up call" | "Decision on Friday: Route 1, Route 2, or both." |
| "Our platform ensures compliance" | "NO PASSPORT = NO EXECUTION." |

## Voice by slide archetype

- **Outline / TOC** — chapter names are two or three words, no verbs: "The wedge." "The proof." "The ask."
- **Themed rows** — row label ALL CAPS Inter; the fact in plain prose; the action in bold italic serif.
- **Two-column contrast** — parallel grammar on both sides, or the contrast reads as noise.
- **Hero word** — one word plus a period ("Sovereignty."), caption beneath does the explaining.
- **Pull quote** — real words from a real person, attributed; never invented praise.
- **Closing CTA** — the decision you are asking for, dated. Not an invitation to keep talking.
